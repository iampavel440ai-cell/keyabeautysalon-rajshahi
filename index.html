Getting ready for a wedding in Rajshahi: what to book and when

Wedding work in Rajshahi runs in waves. From the middle of November the phone at most parlours in Upposhor stays busy, and by the first week of December the good slots for a Saturday evening are gone. If your family is planning a biye or a holud this season, the order in which you book things matters more than how early you start.

Start with the hair, not the face

Most brides call a salon two weeks before the wedding and ask for everything at once. That is usually the wrong order. Colour, keratin and rebonding need time to settle, and a fresh rebonding done three days before the event can look stiff in photographs. Salons in Rajshahi will normally suggest two to four weeks between a chemical hair service and the wedding day, which gives the hair time to relax into its own shape.

If you are only having your hair set on the day, a week is fine. If you want smoother hair for the whole season, plan the keratin rebonding in Rajshahi a month out and keep the last two weeks free for nothing but a wash and a trim.

Book the trial before you book the day

A makeup trial is not a luxury. Rajshahi is hot for most of the year, and a base that photographs well in a cooled room can slide by the time the groom's side reaches the gate. A trial lets you see the base, the eye work and the hair together under the lights you will actually be sitting under, and it lets the artist adjust the shade for your skin rather than guessing on the morning itself.

Bring the outfit or a photo of it, the jewellery if it is already at home, and one person whose opinion you actually trust. Ask how long the artist expects to need on the day, because a bridal look with hair, eye work and drape takes time and the family schedule usually runs late by an hour.

Skin work goes earlier still

Facials and cleanups are better started six to eight weeks before, spaced two to three weeks apart. A hydrafacial or a deep cleanup the day before a wedding is a common mistake, since skin can stay flushed for a day or two. Book the last facial at least a week out and leave the final days for sleep, water and a light moisturiser.

For brides who want a facial close to the date, the safer choice is a short hydrating session rather than anything with extractions. You can read more about the hydrafacial treatment and what it involves before you decide.

What to ask on the phone

Three questions save most of the trouble. Ask whether the artist will be working only on you that morning or on several brides, ask what time they want you in the chair, and ask what the booking needs to hold the slot. A written confirmation is normal and worth keeping, especially in the busy weeks.

If the wedding is a family affair with several sisters or cousins getting ready together, say so when you call. Group bookings need two artists and a longer window, and it is much easier to arrange that a month ahead than on the morning of the holud.
